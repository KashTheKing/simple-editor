Audio page

The Audio page (Alt+5) has a small Preview, the Mixer (with Subtitles behind it), the Inspector, and a tall timeline so audio waveforms are easy to read.

Clip audio​

Select an audio clip (or a video clip's sound) and the Inspector's Audio section shows:

  • Volume (−60 to +12 dB) and Pan, each with a ◆ to keyframe it, and Fade in / Fade out.
  • Role: tag it Dialogue, Music, SFX or Ambience.
  • Bus: which Mixer bus the clip plays through.

Right-click the Audio title for one-click fixes: Repair adds a clean-up chain (high-pass, de-hum, noise gate, compressor, limiter) and Clarity a presence boost, both on a new Mixer bus so you can adjust them; Open in Mixer; and Duck Music under Dialogue and Normalize Selection (below).

The Mixer​

The Mixer (Ctrl+0) has one strip per bus. Every track feeds Main until you route it somewhere else. Each strip has its name, a level meter with a loudness readout (LUFS), the gain fader, pan, M (mute) and S (solo). The ◆ next to gain and pan keyframes them; right-click a ◆ to clear its keyframes.

  • Add a bus with the slim + strip on the right (or right-click an empty spot ▸ Add bus).
  • Right-click a strip for Rename (or double-click the name), Mono, Output (which bus it feeds), Add filter (EQ, high-pass, low-pass, reverb, echo, distortion, compressor, noise gate, noise, gain, de-hum, limiter, de-esser), Show routing and Delete bus.
  • A bus's filters are listed under it: the checkbox switches one off, a click on its name shows its settings (EQs and filters draw their curve). Right-click a filter to open it in its own window, move it up or down, or remove it.

To route tracks to a bus: right-click ▸ Show routing, click the bus's strip, then click the tracks that should go to it. A single clip can also go to its own bus, from the Inspector's Bus menu or the clip's right-click ▸ Audio ▸ Bus.

Ducking and loudness​

  • Duck Music under Dialogue lowers the music wherever someone speaks. First open Window ▸ More ▸ Auto-cut, select the clips, and in its Duck section mark which clip is the Music and which are Dialogue; set the depth and ramp, then Apply Ducking. After that the menu command uses the same choice.
  • Normalize Selection raises or lowers each selected clip so its loudest peak is at −1 dB.
  • Match Loudness across Selection evens out several clips so they sound equally loud.

All three are under Timeline ▸ Auto and the clip's right-click ▸ Audio. Clips with keyframed volume are left alone. The Auto-cut panel's Loudness section shows each clip's peak and RMS level and lets you pick your own target.

Every export is also loudness-normalised to −14 LUFS by default, the level YouTube and most platforms use (turn it off on the Export page).

Record a voiceover​

  1. Put the playhead where the voiceover should start.
  2. File ▸ Record ▸ Record Voiceover… (Ctrl+Alt+R).
  3. Pick your microphone under Input, and Mono or Stereo.
  4. Leave Record from the playhead ticked: the video plays while you talk, so you can time your words.
  5. Click Record, speak, then Stop. The take lands on the timeline at the start point.

Didn't like it? Retake removes the last take and records again from the same spot. Recording needs FFmpeg.

File ▸ Record ▸ Screen Recording… records your desktop (or a region of it), optionally with your microphone, desktop sound and mouse cursor. Screen recordings go into the Library.
