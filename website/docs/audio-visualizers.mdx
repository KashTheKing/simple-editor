![Neon Orb, Spectrum Bars, Voxel Blocks and a custom style on the same song](/img/tutorial/visualizer-styles.jpg)

A visualizer is a layer that moves with the music. Right-click a song in the **Media Library** and pick
**Add with Audio Visualizer**: the song goes on the timeline at the playhead with a full-frame visualizer layer
above it, exactly as long. The other ways in:

- Drag a song from the **Media Library** or the **Media Browser** onto the timeline with **Ctrl+Shift** held.
- Right-click a song already on the timeline ▸ **Add Audio Visualizer**.
- **Gallery ▸ Audio ▸ Visualizers**: click a type to add one at the playhead, or drag it onto a video track.
  This one is not tied to a song: it shows **everything the timeline plays** under it (every audio track, as
  mixed), and is analysed again in the background after each edit.
- **Ctrl+P** ▸ *Add Audio Visualizer* (for the selected song).

Either way the two are **linked**, like a video and its own sound: move, trim or delete one and
the other follows, and exporting the timeline renders the visualizer with the song. A new visualizer gets the
style you picked last. The sound is analysed **once** when the visualizer is made (loudness, six frequency bands from bass to
treble, and the beats), and the result is stored as keyframes on the layer. Playback and export only read
those keyframes, so a visualizer never makes playback stutter, and the export looks exactly like the preview.

## Picking a style

Select the visualizer clip. The Inspector's **Visualizer** section has a **Type** list, **Import shader…** (load
a GLSL visualizer file: it is checked and joins your types), and a **Source**: the song it is linked to, or
**Timeline mix**. Switch a linked one to Timeline mix to make it follow everything that plays.

![The Visualizer section in the Inspector](/img/tutorial/visualizer-inspector.png)

| Style | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Neon Orb** | a glowing, rippling orb in a dark void that swells and boils with the loudness |
| **Spectrum Bars** | a mirrored field of thin blue and green bars receding to a horizon, bass in the middle (the Source monitor's original visualizer) |
| **Voxel Blocks** | a blocky pixel-art island: columns rise with the spectrum, a block drops in and stacks on every beat while the oldest ones break away, and the camera slowly orbits |
| your own | any shader in your library that reads audio (below), marked *(custom)* |

Changing the style swaps the effect and reads the audio again. Moved or trimmed the clip? Press **Re-bake**.
The style's own knobs are in the clip's **Effects** section like any effect. Voxel Blocks has:

- **Palette**: 0 meadow (grass, dirt, stone, ores), 1 ember (red rock, glowing ore), 2 snow, 3 desert.
- **Speed**: how fast the camera orbits (0 = still). **Camera**: 0 low and close to 1 high and wide.
- **Background**: 0 transparent (the island floats over whatever is on the tracks below), 1 sky, 2 black.

![Voxel Blocks palettes, a black background, and a transparent background](/img/tutorial/visualizer-voxel-palettes.jpg)

The **Source** monitor shows a visualizer too, for audio files without cover art. Right-click it ▸ **Audio
Visualizer** to pick the style or turn it off.

## Tuning it to the song: the band mixer

Every song hits differently: an EDM drop lives in the sub-bass, a rock song in its snare and guitars, a ballad
in the voice. Under the style, the Visualizer section has a band mixer titled **Frequency bands**:

- A **curve** over the song's spectrum: frequency left to right, weight bottom to top. Drag a band's point up
  to make it drive the visualizer harder, down to ignore it; double-click puts it back. Six bands from **Sub** (20-80 Hz) through **Bass**
  (80-250), **Low-mid** (250-500), **Mid** (0.5-2 kHz) and **High-mid** (2-6 kHz) to **Air** (6-16 kHz). The
  dim bars are what the song has, the bright ones what the visualizer will react to.
- **Intensity** per band: 0 ignores the band, 1 is normal, up to 3 boosts it.
- **Beat source**: tick the bands the beat detection listens to - the kick lives in Sub / Bass (EDM, hip-hop), the
  snare in Mid (rock). Nothing ticked = the whole sound.
- **Sensitivity** scales everything; **Attack** is how fast it rises to a hit (0 = instantly), **Release** how
  slowly it falls back (short = punchy, long = smooth).
- **Preset**: presets for EDM, Hip-hop, Rock, Vocal, Lo-fi and Classical as quick starting points, and
  **Reset** for every band equal.

Every change shows at once, playing or paused, and is one undo step. The song is analysed once and kept, so
tuning never makes playback stutter. The built-in styles use it as they are: Neon Orb pulses with the mixed
level, Spectrum Bars and Voxel Blocks raise each band by its own intensity. The mix is saved with the project
and stays when you change the style.

## Hearing a cut without the picture: Audio Only

Right-click the **Preview** ▸ **Audio Only** (also in the **Playback** menu and **Ctrl+P**) to play the timeline's
sound with a visualizer of the whole mix in place of the picture. No video is decoded, so it is light even on a
heavy timeline. The visualizer uses the style you picked last, follows the mix at the playhead, and an **Audio
Only** badge sits in the corner. The setting is saved with the project; exports always have the picture.

## Flying the camera (3D styles)

Voxel Blocks (and any custom style marked as 3D, below) has a real camera. With the visualizer clip selected
and the playhead on it:

- In the **Preview**: **drag** to orbit around the scene, **Shift+drag** to pan, the **scroll wheel** to move
  closer or further. (Ctrl+scroll and middle-drag still zoom and pan the viewer itself.)
- In the Inspector's **Camera** section: **Yaw**, **Pitch**, **Distance**, **Field of view**, **Roll** and the
  **Target** it looks at, each with its ◆ to set a keyframe at the playhead - key a few positions for a
  fly-through. **Auto-orbit** keeps the slow turn going (its speed in degrees a second) on top of what you set.
  **Reset camera** goes back to the default orbit.

Moving the camera only changes what the shader is told, so it never costs playback frames, and the preview
and the export use the same camera at every moment. The camera is saved with the project and stays when you
change the style.

## Making your own visualizer

A visualizer style is a [shader effect](effects.mdx) (GLSL, the same format as the library's effects) that
declares any of these controls in its header. When a visualizer is made, they are filled in from the music:

| Control | Header line | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `level` | `// @param level 0.3 0 1` | loudness as the band mixer hears it (the intensity-weighted sum of the bands, times the sensitivity), mapped onto the control's range |
| `band1` .. `band6` | `// @param band1 0.5 0 1` | the six bands Sub, Bass, Low-mid, Mid, High-mid, Air, each times its intensity and the sensitivity, mapped onto its range |
| `beat` | `// @param beat 0 0 1` | 0 on each beat the band mixer's beat bands hear, rising to 1 just before the next |
| `beats` | `// @param beats 0 0 100000` | how many beats have passed: 0, then 1, 2, 3, ... |
| `bpm` | `// @param bpm 120 0 300` | the song's tempo |

A 3D shader can take the visualizer camera too: add the header line `// @camera` and it gets

| Uniform | What |
|---|---|
| `vec3 u_cam_pos` | where the camera is, in your scene's own units (the Target is the origin by default) |
| `vec3 u_cam_target` | the point it looks at |
| `float u_fov` | vertical field of view, radians |
| `float u_cam_roll` | roll around the view axis, radians |

Build your ray from them (forward = `normalize(u_cam_target - u_cam_pos)`, a focal length of
`0.5 / tan(u_fov / 2)` for a `uv` spanning one unit of height). Library thumbnails render without a camera
(the two points are equal), so fall back to a default view when `u_cam_pos == u_cam_target`, as
`library/effects/voxel-blocks.glsl` does.

Everything else in the header is a normal knob. Give the audio controls defaults that look good on their
own: the library thumbnail and the Source monitor (without sound) use them. A shader may have up to 14
controls.

```glsl
// @name Pulse Rings
// @desc Six neon rings, bass in the middle to treble outside.
// @tags visualizer, audio
// @param level 0.3 0 1
// @param band1 0.5 0 1
// @param band6 0.25 0 1
// @param beat 0 0 1
vec4 effect(vec4 src, vec2 uv) {
    vec2 p = (uv - 0.5) * vec2(u_res.x / u_res.y, 1.0);
    float r = length(p);
    float ring = exp(-pow((r - 0.1 - 0.05 * band1) / 0.01, 2.0)) + exp(-pow((r - 0.3 - 0.05 * band6) / 0.01, 2.0));
    float flash = exp(-beat * 6.0);              // bright on every beat, fading until the next
    return vec4(vec3(1.0, 0.3, 0.8) * ring * (0.5 + level + flash), 1.0);
}
```

Some ideas with the beat inputs: `floor(beats)` counts hits, so `mod(floor(beats), 8.0)` cycles through 8
states and `hash11(floor(beats))` picks something new on every beat (Voxel Blocks drops each block onto the
column `hash(beats)` picks); `beat` animates within one beat, so `1.0 - beat` is a decay and
`smoothstep(0.0, 0.3, beat)` a drop that lands a third of the way in.

To install it, save the file into `%APPDATA%\SimpleEditor\library\effects` (or ask the Assistant to save it
with `library.save`, kind `effect`). It then appears in the Visualizer **Style** list as *(custom)*. Use the
Shader Editor's log, or `shader.check`, to fix compile errors first. A complete sample:
`tools/visualizer-sample/pulse-rings.glsl`.

## Now playing

![The now-playing card: cover art and tags, or the music-note icon](/img/tutorial/visualizer-now-playing.jpg)

When only sound is playing, with no picture and no visualizer (a song in the Source monitor with the
visualizer off, or a stretch of the timeline with only audio), the monitor shows the song's **cover art**
(the picture embedded in the file) and its **title and artist**, or the music-note icon and the file name when
the file has neither. The tags and picture are read once per file in the background. The card shows only in
the monitors; it is not part of the export.

## For the Assistant (MCP)

| Tool | Does |
|---|---|
| `audio.visualizer {clip_id, style, preset, gains, sensitivity, attack, release, beat_bands}` | adds a visualizer over an audio clip (or `asset_id`: a song with its visualizer), restyles a visualizer clip, or sets its band mixer |
| `audio.visualizer_styles` | lists the styles, built-in and custom |
| `audio.visualizer {clip_id, camera, camera_time, camera_key}` | sets a 3D style's camera (yaw, pitch, distance, fov, roll, target, auto_orbit, orbit_speed), keyed at a time for fly-throughs |
| `audio.bake_keys {clip_id, target_id, effect, param}` | bakes a clip's loudness into any effect parameter |
