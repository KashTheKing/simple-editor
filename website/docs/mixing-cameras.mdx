---
title: Mixing cameras and frame rates
description: 4K 50p and 1080p 25p in one edit, slow motion from high frame rates, punching in on 4K, proxies and matching two cameras' colour.
---

A common question: *"I shot the chorus on a Sony A7 IV in 4K 50p and the verses on an a6400 in 1080p 25p.
Will mixing them cause trouble, and what's the workflow?"*

In Simple Editor it just works. Import everything and follow the cards.

## 1. Import the whole shoot

Drop all the clips in at once. Simple Editor looks at every video, not just the first, and shows one line:

> Mixed footage: 1080p at 25 fps — your 50 fps clips can play as smooth half-speed slow motion

Hover it to see what it counted and why. Click **Match** (one undo step), or **4K** first to deliver 4K.

- **Frame rate**: it picks the rate the others divide into evenly. 25 for 25 + 50, 30 for 30 + 60, 24 for
  24 + 48. Every clip then plays whole frames: at normal speed a 50p clip shows every 2nd frame, never a
  blend, so motion stays crisp.
- **Mixes that don't divide evenly** (24 with 30, 25 with 29.97) get the most common rate and the warning
  *some clips will drop or repeat frames*. See [Odd frame rates](#odd-frame-rates).
- **Size**: 1080p, unless every clip is 4K. You choose 1080p or 4K on the card.

## 2. Slow motion from 50p

A clip whose rate is a whole multiple of the project's shows a small rate badge on the timeline (**50**).
Right-click it ▸ **Speed** ▸ **Slow motion (every frame)**, or pick **Every frame** in the Inspector's
**Preset** row (**Normal**, **Every frame**, **Custom**). Each of the clip's frames becomes one project frame:
50p in a 25p project is exactly 50 % speed, perfectly smooth, nothing blended. The clip grows to play its
whole shot when there is room after it; otherwise it keeps its length and you drag its end for the rest.

## 3. Punch in on 4K

A 4K clip in a 1080p project fits the whole frame by default. To reframe, use **Zoom** in the Inspector's
Transform section: 100 % is the full frame, and the mark on the slider is **1:1** (one camera pixel per
project pixel). **Max sharp** says where that is: 200 % for 4K in 1080p. Up to there a punch-in loses
nothing; past it the picture is upscaled.

A clip smaller than the project (720p in 1080p) is enlarged even at 100 %, and the Inspector says
**Upscaled**. If the AI enhancer is installed, the **AI Upscale** button next to it makes a sharper copy.

## Odd frame rates

For a 24p clip in a 30p project (or any mix that doesn't divide evenly), the Inspector shows **Frame rate**:

| Choice | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Frame sampling** | The nearest real frame. Sharp; some frames repeat or drop. The default. |
| **Frame blending** | Mixes neighbouring frames. Smoother, with a little ghosting. |
| **Optical flow** | Renders new in-between frames from the motion with ffmpeg in the background, then swaps the clip onto the result. Best motion, takes a while. |

Optical flow is never worked out while you play: playback always stays smooth.

## Heavy footage

4K long-GOP files (H.264 / HEVC straight from the camera) and 10-bit HEVC can be too much to decode in real
time. 4K gets proxies automatically while **Settings ▸ Performance ▸ Play low-res all-intra proxies in the preview** is on. Otherwise a card asks
*This 4K footage may stutter — make proxies?*; your answer is remembered, and **Settings ▸ Performance ▸
Heavy footage** changes it (Ask, Make proxies, Play originals). Proxies are only for editing: exports always use
the original files.

## Two cameras, one look

Simple Editor reads the camera make and model from each file. When the timeline holds clips from two or
more cameras, a card offers **Match colours between cameras**: pick the reference camera and the other
cameras' clips are colour matched to it ([Colour Match](color.mdx)), as one undo step. It is never done
for you.

Footage shot in **S-Log3** or **S-Log2** (found in the file's metadata or its name) gets a card too: **Add LUT**
puts the matching conversion LUT from the library (S-Log3 to Rec.709, S-Log2 to Rec.709) on those clips' sources,
so every clip cut from them is converted before your grade.

HLG footage that the file says is HLG needs nothing: it is converted when it is decoded. HLG that is only named
in the metadata or the file name (the stream itself is not marked HLG) gets the same card with an HLG to Rec.709
LUT.
