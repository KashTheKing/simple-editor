![The Audio page](/img/tutorial/page-audio.png)

The **Audio** page (<kbd>Alt+6</kbd>) has everything for sound work in one place:

- a **music** area: the Library (showing your audio files when you open the page) and the Gallery's
  **Audio** side (sound effects, audio animations, crossfades, bus effects),
- a small **Preview**,
- the **Mixer** with the **Voiceover** and **Transcript** panels behind it,
- the **Inspector** for the selected clip (an audio visualizer's controls are there too),
- a tall timeline so waveforms are easy to read, with the Beat Aligner tabbed beside it.

If you had already changed the Audio page, your arrangement is kept: **Window ▸ Layout ▸ Reset Page Layout**
gives you the new one.

## Clip audio

Select an audio clip (or a video clip's sound) and the Inspector's **Audio** section shows:

- **Volume** (−60 to +12 dB) and **Pan**, each with a **◆** to [keyframe](inspector.mdx#keyframes) it, and
  **Fade in** / **Fade out**.
- **Role**: tag it Dialogue, Music, SFX or Ambience.
- **Bus**: which Mixer bus the clip plays through.

Right-click the **Audio** title for one-click fixes: **Repair** adds a clean-up chain (high-pass, de-hum,
noise gate, compressor, limiter) and **Clarity** a presence boost, both on a new Mixer bus so you can
adjust them; **Open in Mixer**; and **Duck Music under Dialogue** and **Normalize Selection** (below).

## Clean Audio

**Clean Audio** in the Inspector's **Audio** section removes background noise from speech: fans, air
conditioning, traffic, keyboard clicks and room hum.

- **Clean** cleans the selected clips (a video clip's sound too). It runs in the background in the
  **Jobs** pane, where you can cancel it, and only works on the part of the file the clips use, plus a second
  either side for trims. When it is done the clips play the cleaned sound; their timing, volume, fades and
  keyframes stay the same.
- **Strength** (0–100 %) sets how much of the noise goes. 100 % removes all of it; a little lower keeps some
  of the room so a voice sounds natural. Change it and click **Clean** again.
- **A** / **B** switches between the original (**A**) and the cleaned sound (**B**), so you can compare them
  while it plays. Each switch is one undo step.

Your original file is never changed. The cleaned sound is a WAV file in the app's cache folder, shown in the
Library's **Cleaned audio** bin. Cleaning the same part at the same strength again reuses it at once.

### Speech denoise add-on

Clean Audio works best with **speech denoise**, an optional add-on that uses
[DeepFilterNet](https://github.com/Rikorose/DeepFilterNet) (MIT / Apache-2.0), a speech model that runs on the
processor. Install it from **Settings ▸ General ▸ Speech denoise ▸ Install**, or the **Install speech
denoise** button under Clean Audio. It is about 27 MB, downloaded once and checked before it is used; nothing
else is sent anywhere. **Remove** takes it off again.

Without the add-on, Clean Audio and the Denoise bake use ffmpeg's built-in `afftdn` filter, which is fine on
steady hiss but weaker on everything else.

On a test recording (a voice over loud pink noise), the noise floor went from −28.6 dB to −57.4 dB with
DeepFilterNet at full strength, against −29.5 dB with `afftdn`.

For assistants and scripts: `audio.clean` (with `engine`, `strength`, or `ab` to switch A / B),
`audio.repair` with preset `clean`, `export.denoise` (with `engine` and `strength`), and `denoise.status` /
`denoise.install`. `engine` is `deepfilter` or `ffmpeg`; the default is `deepfilter` when the add-on is
installed.

## The Mixer

![The Mixer](/img/tutorial/mixer.png)

The **Mixer** (<kbd>Ctrl+0</kbd>) has one strip per **bus**. Every track feeds **Main** until you route it
somewhere else. Each strip has its name, a level meter with a loudness readout (LUFS), the **gain** fader,
**pan**, **M** (mute) and **S** (solo). The ◆ next to gain and pan keyframes them; right-click a ◆ to clear
its keyframes.

- **Add a bus** with the slim **+** strip on the right (or right-click an empty spot ▸ **Add bus**).
- **Right-click a strip** for **Rename** (or double-click the name), **Mono**, **Output** (which bus it feeds),
  **Add filter** (EQ, high-pass, low-pass, reverb, echo, distortion, compressor, noise gate, noise, gain,
  de-hum, limiter, de-esser), **Show routing** and **Delete bus**.
- A bus's **filters** are listed under it: the checkbox switches one off, a click on its name shows its
  settings (EQs and filters draw their curve). Right-click a filter to open it in its own window, move it
  up or down, or remove it.

**To route tracks to a bus**: right-click ▸ **Show routing**, click the bus's strip, then click the tracks
that should go to it. A single clip can also go to its own bus, from the Inspector's **Bus** menu or the
clip's right-click ▸ **Audio ▸ Bus**.

Right-click an audio clip ▸ **Audio ▸ Add Audio Visualizer** for a layer that moves with the music. See

## Ducking and loudness

- **Duck Music under Dialogue** lowers the music wherever someone speaks. First open **Window ▸ More ▸
  Auto-cut**, select the clips, and in its **Duck** section mark which clip is the **Music** and which are
  **Dialogue**; set the depth and ramp, then **Apply Ducking**. After that the menu command uses the same choice.
- **Normalize Selection** raises or lowers each selected clip so its loudest peak is at −1 dB.
- **Match Loudness across Selection** evens out several clips so they sound equally loud.

All three are under **Timeline ▸ Auto** and the clip's right-click ▸ **Audio**. Clips with keyframed volume
are left alone. The Auto-cut panel's **Loudness** section shows each clip's peak and RMS level and lets
you pick your own target.

Every export is also loudness-normalised to −14 LUFS by default, the level YouTube and most platforms
use (turn it off on the [Export page](export.mdx)).

## Record a voiceover

The **Voiceover** panel is on the Audio page behind the Mixer, and anywhere else from **File ▸ Record ▸
Record Voiceover** (<kbd>Ctrl+Alt+R</kbd>), the Window menu or <kbd>Ctrl+P</kbd>.

1. Put the playhead where the voiceover should start.
2. Pick your microphone under **Input**, and **Mono** or **Stereo**.
3. Leave **Punch in at the playhead** ticked: the video plays while you talk, so you can time your words.
4. Click **Record**, speak (the meter shows your level), then **Stop**. The take lands on the timeline at
   the start point.

Didn't like it? **Retake** removes the last take and records again from the same spot. Every take on the
timeline is listed under the buttons: click its time to jump there, or **×** to delete it. Recording needs
FFmpeg.

To record your screen, a window or a game with its sound, see [Screen recording](screen-recording.mdx).
