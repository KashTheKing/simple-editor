![The Audio page](/img/tutorial/page-audio.png)

The **Audio** page (<kbd>Alt+6</kbd>) has everything for sound work in one place:

- a **music** area: the Library (showing your audio files when you open the page) and the Gallery's
  **Audio** side (sound effects, audio animations, crossfades, bus effects),
- a small **Preview**,
- the **Mixer** with the **Voiceover** and **Transcript** panels behind it,
- the **Inspector** for the selected clip (an audio visualizer's controls are there too),
- a tall timeline so waveforms are easy to read, with the Beat Aligner tabbed beside it.

If you had already changed the Audio page, your arrangement is kept: **Window ▸ Layout ▸ Reset Page Layout**
gives you the new one.

## Clip audio

Select an audio clip (or a video clip's sound) and the Inspector's **Audio** section shows:

- **Volume** (−60 to +12 dB) and **Pan**, each with a **◆** to [keyframe](inspector.mdx#keyframes) it, and
  **Fade in** / **Fade out**.
- **Role**: tag it Dialogue, Music, SFX or Ambience.
- **Bus**: which Mixer bus the clip plays through.
- **Filters**: the clip's own audio filters. **Add** lists every filter the Mixer has; they run on this clip
  before its volume, pan and fades, so a fade also fades the reverb. They list, open and reorder exactly
  like a bus's filters (below), and their settings can be keyframed.

Right-click the **Audio** title for one-click fixes: **Repair** adds a clean-up chain (high-pass, de-hum,
noise gate, compressor, limiter) and **Clarity** a presence boost, both on a new Mixer bus so you can
adjust them; **Open in Mixer**; and **Duck Music under Dialogue** and **Normalize Selection** (below).

## Clean Audio

**Clean Audio** in the Inspector's **Audio** section removes background noise from speech: fans, air
conditioning, traffic, keyboard clicks and room hum.

- **Clean** cleans the selected clips (a video clip's sound too). It runs in the background in the
  **Jobs** pane, where you can cancel it, and only works on the part of the file the clips use, plus a second
  either side for trims. When it is done the clips play the cleaned sound; their timing, volume, fades and
  keyframes stay the same.
- **Strength** (0–100 %) sets how much of the noise goes. 100 % removes all of it; a little lower keeps some
  of the room so a voice sounds natural. Change it and click **Clean** again.
- **A** / **B** switches between the original (**A**) and the cleaned sound (**B**), so you can compare them
  while it plays. Each switch is one undo step.

Your original file is never changed. The cleaned sound is a WAV file in the app's cache folder, shown in the
Library's **Cleaned audio** bin. Cleaning the same part at the same strength again reuses it at once.

### Speech denoise add-on

Clean Audio works best with **speech denoise**, an optional add-on that uses
[DeepFilterNet](https://github.com/Rikorose/DeepFilterNet) (MIT / Apache-2.0), a speech model that runs on the
processor. Install it from **Settings ▸ General ▸ Speech denoise ▸ Install**, or the **Install speech
denoise** button under Clean Audio. It is about 27 MB, downloaded once and checked before it is used; nothing
else is sent anywhere. **Remove** takes it off again.

Without the add-on, Clean Audio and the Denoise bake use ffmpeg's built-in `afftdn` filter, which is fine on
steady hiss but weaker on everything else.

On a test recording (a voice over loud pink noise), the noise floor went from −28.6 dB to −57.4 dB with
DeepFilterNet at full strength, against −29.5 dB with `afftdn`.

For assistants and scripts: `audio.clean` (with `engine`, `strength`, or `ab` to switch A / B),
`audio.repair` with preset `clean`, `export.denoise` (with `engine` and `strength`), and `denoise.status` /
`denoise.install`. `engine` is `deepfilter` or `ffmpeg`; the default is `deepfilter` when the add-on is
installed.

## The Mixer

![The Mixer](/img/tutorial/mixer.png)

The **Mixer** (<kbd>Ctrl+0</kbd>) has one strip per **bus**. Every track feeds **Main** until you route it
somewhere else. Each strip has its name, a level meter with a loudness readout (LUFS), the **gain** fader,
**pan**, **M** (mute) and **S** (solo). The ◆ next to gain and pan keyframes them; right-click a ◆ to clear
its keyframes.

- **Add a bus** with the slim **+** strip on the right (or right-click an empty spot ▸ **Add bus**).
- **Right-click a strip** for **Rename** (or double-click the name), **Mono**, **Output** (which bus it feeds),
  **Add filter** (EQ, 15-band EQ, high-pass, low-pass, band-pass, notch, reverb, echo, distortion,
  compressor, expander, noise gate, limiter, de-esser, de-hum, de-click, mid-side, stereo width, channels
  (copy, swap or invert left and right), downmix, two-pass normalize, pitch, noise, gain), **Show routing**
  and **Delete bus**.
- A bus's **filters** are listed under it: the checkbox switches one off, a click on its name shows its
  settings (EQs and filters draw their curve). Right-click a filter to open it in its own window, move it
  up or down, or remove it.

**To route tracks to a bus**: right-click ▸ **Show routing**, click the bus's strip, then click the tracks
that should go to it. A single clip can also go to its own bus, from the Inspector's **Bus** menu or the
clip's right-click ▸ **Audio ▸ Bus**.

**Two-pass normalize**: add **Normalize (two-pass)** to a clip, set **Target LUFS** (-16 for streaming,
-23 for broadcast) and click **Analyze**. The clip is measured in the background; the filter then applies
the difference, up to its **Max gain**. Analyze again after changing the filters before it.

**Auto Fade and Smooth Seams**: right-click an audio track's header. **Auto Fade** puts a 10 ms fade at
every clip edge that has no fade or transition, so cuts never click. **Smooth Seams** cross-fades clips that
touch over 10 ms, so a cut in the middle of continuous sound is seamless.

## Audio Scopes

On the Audio page, the **Audio Scopes** tab beside the Mixer (or **Window ▸ Audio Scopes**) shows what is
playing: a **spectrum** from 20 Hz to 20 kHz (hover a bar for its frequency and level), a **vectorscope**
(mono is a vertical line, wide stereo a cloud, out-of-phase sound a horizontal line) and a **correlation**
meter (+1 mono, 0 wide, below 0 the channels cancel when played in mono).

## Tone generator

**Clip ▸ Add ▸ Tone Generator…** makes a sine, square, sawtooth or triangle tone, or white or pink noise, at
the frequency, level and length you choose, adds it to the Library and places it at the playhead.

## Visualizers

Right-click an audio clip ▸ **Audio ▸ Add Audio Visualizer** for a layer that moves with the music. See
[Audio visualizers](audio-visualizers.mdx).

## Ducking and loudness

- **Duck Music under Dialogue** lowers the music wherever someone speaks. First open **Window ▸ More ▸
  Auto-cut**, select the clips, and in its **Duck** section mark which clip is the **Music** and which are
  **Dialogue**; set the depth and ramp, then **Apply Ducking**. After that the menu command uses the same choice.
- **Normalize Selection** raises or lowers each selected clip so its loudest peak is at −1 dB.
- **Match Loudness across Selection** evens out several clips so they sound equally loud.

All three are under **Timeline ▸ Auto** and the clip's right-click ▸ **Audio**. Clips with keyframed volume
are left alone. The Auto-cut panel's **Loudness** section shows each clip's peak and RMS level and lets
you pick your own target.

Every export is also loudness-normalised to −14 LUFS by default, the level YouTube and most platforms
use (turn it off on the [Export page](export.mdx)).

## Stem separation

Split a song (or a video's sound) into four stems: **drums**, **bass**, **other** and **vocals**. It uses
[Demucs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs) (the htdemucs model), an optional add-on you install
once: **Settings ▸ General ▸ Stem Separation ▸ Install** (about 85 MB; the first Separate offers it too).
It runs on the processor, below normal priority and on half the cores, so playback stays smooth while it
works. It takes a while (minutes for a song, longer on older processors), but each song is separated only once.

Right-click an audio or video clip ▸ **Audio ▸ Stems** (or right-click the Inspector's **Audio** section):

- **Separate Stems** puts the four stems in the Library, in a **Stems · *song name*** bin.
- **Separate Stems to Tracks** also lays them on four new audio tracks (**Drums**, **Bass**, **Other**,
  **Vocals**) right below the clip's track, lined up with the clip and given the
  same trims, speed, volume and fades, and switches the original's sound off (**Enable** brings it back).
- **Remove Vocals** does the same with the vocals stem off: an instrumental.
- **Keep Only Vocals** does the same with only the vocals on.

The job shows in the **Jobs** panel, where you can cancel it. Stems are kept in the app's cache (never
next to your files), so separating the same song again is instant; changing the file separates it afresh.

- **Duck just the vocals**: once a song's stems are on tracks, **Duck Music under Dialogue** lowers only its vocals stem
  (whichever of the song's clips you picked as the music). The singing dips under your voiceover and the
  beat keeps going.
- **Beats from the drums**: once a song is separated, the Beat Aligner's **Detect** reads its drums stem,
  which gives a cleaner tempo and grid on a busy mix.

Licences: demucs.cpp (the C++ port the add-on runs) is MIT, Demucs and its htdemucs weights are MIT (Meta
publishes no separate licence for the weights), Eigen is MPL-2.0 and libnyquist BSD-2-Clause.

## Record a voiceover

The **Voiceover** panel is on the Audio page behind the Mixer, and anywhere else from **File ▸ Record ▸
Record Voiceover** (<kbd>Ctrl+Alt+R</kbd>), the Window menu or <kbd>Ctrl+P</kbd>.

1. Put the playhead where the voiceover should start.
2. Pick your microphone under **Input**, and **Mono** or **Stereo**.
3. Leave **Punch in at the playhead** ticked: the video plays while you talk, so you can time your words.
4. Click **Record**, speak (the meter shows your level), then **Stop**. The take lands on the timeline at
   the start point.

Didn't like it? **Retake** removes the last take and records again from the same spot. Every take on the
timeline is listed under the buttons: click its time to jump there, or **×** to delete it. Recording needs
FFmpeg.

To record your screen, a window or a game with its sound, see [Screen recording](screen-recording.mdx).

## Text to speech

The **Speech** section sits under the takes in the **Voiceover** panel (the Transcript panel's **⋯ ▸ Speak
text…** opens it too). Pick an **Engine**:

| Engine | What it is | Download |
|---|---|---|
| **Windows** | The voices built into Windows. Always there. | none |
| **Kokoro** | Natural voices (28 American and British English voices) that run on the processor. | about 160 MB |
| **Chatterbox** | Expressive voices with an **Intensity** slider, and voice cloning. Fastest with a graphics card (DirectML), works on the processor too. | about 1.6 GB |

Kokoro and Chatterbox are add-ons: click **Install** next to the engine, or in **Settings ▸ General**, where
**Remove** deletes them again and shows how much space they take. Until an add-on is installed, Speak uses a
Windows voice and tells you so. When an install finishes while Windows is the engine, the new add-on becomes
the engine, so Kokoro is the default voice from then on.

1. Pick a **Voice**. **Play** speaks a short sample through your speakers (nothing plays until you click it,
   and it stays silent while the Main bus is muted in the Mixer).
2. Set **Speed** and **Pitch** (in semitones); for Chatterbox also **Intensity** (0.5 is neutral, higher is
   more emotional and a little faster).
3. Type a script, one clip per line. A line that starts with a time (`0:12 Welcome back`) is placed at that
   time; the others go one after another from the playhead.
4. Click **Speak at playhead**. Each line becomes a clip on the **Voiceover** track (made for you).

**Speak selection** speaks the selected text clips (titles, plan cards) at their start and links the sound to
them, or, with transcript words selected in the Transcript panel, speaks those words where they are.

Speech runs as a job in the Jobs panel, one at a time, and can be cancelled (cancelling a script line also
cancels the lines after it). The WAV files are kept in the cache folder, so speaking the same line again is
instant.

### Clone a voice (Chatterbox)

Select 5 to 15 seconds of a voice: a clip in the Library or on the timeline, or record a take first. Type a
**Name**, tick **I have permission to use this voice** and click **Clone** (the box clears after each clone). The voice is saved in `%APPDATA%\SimpleEditor\voices` and shows up in the voice
list; **×** next to the list deletes it. **Only clone voices you have permission to use.**
