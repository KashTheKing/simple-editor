title: Browser window & fake mouse
description: Put your footage inside a drawn browser window, scroll and zoom the page, and record a mouse cursor that clicks around in it.
---

A **browser layer** is a clip that shows everything on the tracks below it inside a browser window:
tabs, an address bar, a shadow, a background around it. A **fake mouse** moves over the page, clicks,
drags and scrolls. Use it for web demos, game-site walkthroughs and tutorials: you decide what the
"browser" shows and what the mouse does, and you can change both afterwards.

The window is drawn by the editor. It needs no real browser and adds almost nothing to memory: the
window art is drawn once and reused, the page is your own clips.

## Add one

- **Clip ▸ Add ▸ Add Browser Window**, or the **Browser Window** card in **Gallery ▸ Titles**.
- The clip goes on a track **above** what is there and lasts to the end of the timeline. Trim it like
  any clip: where it ends, the picture is normal again.
- Put the page under it: a screenshot, a screen recording, an [HTML layer](html-layers.mdx), an imported
  web page (below), several clips at once. Whatever is below is the page.
- Pictures already under it are fitted as pages straight away: as wide as the window, top at the top.
  **Fit pages automatically** in the Inspector turns that off.

Move, scale and rotate the window on the [viewer](viewer.mdx) or in **Transform**, with keyframes if
you like: the page and the mouse move with it.

## Import a real web page

**Clip ▸ Add ▸ Import Web Page…** (or **Import web page…** in the Inspector) takes a page address, or a
page you saved from your browser as a single file (`.mhtml`) or as `.html`.

- The page is saved to this computer with its styles, pictures and fonts. Its scripts are removed and
  nothing is fetched from the internet again later.
- It becomes an [HTML layer](html-layers.mdx) clip under a browser layer (a new one if there is none),
  with the address and tab title filled in. It is the real page, drawn by the editor: effects, masks,
  LUTs, blend modes and keyframes work on it like on any clip, and you can open its code.
- **Make editable** turns the page's headings, button and link labels and short texts into **Fields**
  of that clip (and the page background into a colour): select the page clip and retype them in the
  Inspector.
- The editor also remembers every **link, button and input** on the page: its text and where it is. The
  mouse can then go to them by name (below).

An address is opened the way a visitor who is not signed in sees it. To import a page **as you**, press
**Sign in…** first: it opens Microsoft Edge with a profile kept only for Simple Editor, and you sign in
there yourself. Simple Editor never asks for, reads or stores your password. Close that window, tick
**As me**, and import. A page imported as you shows your own view of the site (your name, your lists):
it stays on this computer unless you share the project or the video.

Some sites load their pictures only for a real, signed-in browser; those pictures stay grey in a page
imported by address. Save the page from your own browser as a single file and import that instead.

## Scroll and zoom without blur

**Window zoom** makes the whole browser bigger - frame, tabs, page and the mouse cursor - like a camera
moving closer to the screen. **Zoom at** is the point it holds still; **In ×2** zooms to where the mouse is.
**Page zoom** enlarges only the page inside an unchanged window. Both are drawn again at the zoomed size, so
nothing is stretched: the window and cursor are redrawn, and an imported page is drawn at each zoom level
it passes through.

**Scroll** and **Zoom** move and enlarge the clips under the browser, not a picture of the frame. A page
with more pixels than the frame (a tall screenshot, an imported page) is shown from its own pixels.

- **Zoom at** is the point the zoom holds still. **In ×2** zooms in to where the mouse cursor is;
  **Out** goes back.
- Zooming into an imported page stays crisp: wherever the zoom rests, that part of the page is drawn
  again from its HTML at exactly that zoom (300 %, 800 % and beyond). This happens by itself a moment
  after you change a zoom; it shows as a **Zoom detail** clip under the browser. **Sharpen zooms** does
  it at once. While the zoom is moving, the picture is enlarged and settles sharp when it rests.

## Browser window or page only

**Display** at the top of the Browser section chooses what is drawn around the page:

- **Browser window**: tabs, address bar, frame, shadow and a background. Each part has its own switch
  under **Show** (an address bar without tabs, for example); set Shadow or Corners to 0 to drop those.
- **Page only**: nothing but the page, filling the frame edge to edge, like a browser in full screen.
  Scroll, zoom, the mouse, hover, clicks and page changes work the same.

## The window

Select the browser clip. The [Inspector](inspector.mdx) has a **Browser** section:

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Look** | Light, Dark, Minimal (one slim bar) or Mobile (a phone-shaped window). |
| **Address** | The text in the address bar. **Type it** makes it type itself out from the playhead. |
| **Tabs** | One row per tab: its title, an optional icon picture, and a dot that puts it in front from the playhead on. |
| **Scroll** | Moves the page up, in pixels. Keyframe it to scroll. |
| **Zoom** | 1 fits the page to the window's width; more zooms in. See below. |
| **Load bar** | The thin bar under the address. **Run** plays it once from the playhead. |
| **Size**, **Corners**, **Shadow** | The window itself. |
| **Show** | Tabs, address bar, window dots, bookmarks bar. |
| **Font** | The font of the window's own text. |
| **Around it** | One colour, two colours (a top-to-bottom gradient) or a picture. |

**Fit pages** makes the pictures below as wide as the window with their top at the top. Do this for a
tall screenshot: **Scroll** then runs down the whole page at the screenshot's own sharpness.

**Next page here** cuts what is below at the playhead and runs the load bar. Replace the clip after the
cut with the next page and you have a page change. It is one undo step.

## The mouse

The **Mouse** section holds the cursor: arrow, hand or text pointer, its size and colours, the click
ripple colour. Three ways to make it move:

1. **Record mouse.** Press it, and the timeline plays. Move your real mouse over the viewer, click, drag,
   turn the wheel. Press **Esc** (or the red label) to stop. The recording becomes a handful of keyframes
   and click marks, not one per frame, so you can still edit it. **Smoothing** sets how much is
   simplified. To redo one part, put the playhead there and record again: only the part you record is
   replaced.
2. **Place waypoints.** Click on the viewer where the cursor should be at the playhead. Each click moves
   the playhead on a little, so a few clicks make a path. **Shift**-click adds a click there.
3. **Keyframes.** **X** and **Y** have the usual ◆ buttons.

**Curved** runs the cursor through its keys on a smooth curve instead of straight lines. **Tidy path**
removes jitter from a path that is already there.

At the playhead you can add a **Click**, **Double click**, **Press** / **Release** (a drag), a
**Wheel** mark, or switch the pointer to a hand or text pointer from there on (for hovering a link or a
text box). **Show keys** shows keys such as `Ctrl+L` in a small overlay.

**Go to it**: type the text of a link or button of an imported page (`Play`, a game's name) and the mouse
moves there, arriving at the playhead. It eases in, runs a little past, and settles, the way a hand does,
and lands on the element wherever the page is scrolled or zoomed to. If the element is out of sight, the
page scrolls to it first.

**Click and go**: choose the next page (any picture in the Library, such as a second imported page) and
press it. In one undo step the mouse goes to the element, turns into a hand, the element lights up, it
clicks at the playhead, the page fades to the next one, the load bar runs, and the address and tab title
become the next page's.

**Press what is under it** makes the clip under the cursor dip and spring back, like a pressed button.
It works on a clip that is a button of its own (a picture, an HTML layer), not on a whole-page
screenshot.

## For scripts and the assistant

Everything above is also a tool, seven in all: `browser.add`, `browser.set` (read the style, or change
it), `browser.animate` (type_url, load, switch_tab, scroll_to, zoom_to), `browser.page` (`action`: fit, next,
click_go, elements, sharpen, make_editable), `browser.import` (`action`: import, jobs, sign_in), `cursor.set`
(`action`: move_to, path, event, press, tidy, samples) and `cursor.record` (`action`: start, stop). See
[Scripting](scripting.mdx).

## Good to know

- The window is not a real browser: links do nothing by themselves. A click is a mark on the timeline;
  what happens next is your next clip.
- A browser layer inside a nested sequence works the same.
- While a browser clip is on screen, the outline handles of the clips below it sit where those clips
  really are, not where the page shows them.
