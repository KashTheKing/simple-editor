![The Playlists panel beside the Library](/img/tutorial/music-playlists.png)

Simple Editor can look after a music collection as well as a video: edit the tags of songs and videos, collect songs
into playlists, play them, make a mix video with a visualizer, and burn an audio CD or a data disc.

## Tags and cover art

Click a song or a video in the **Library** and the **Inspector** shows its **Tags**:

- **Music**: Title, Artist, Album, Album artist, Genre, Year, Track, Disc, Comment and Lyrics, plus the **cover art**.
- **Video**: Title, Genre, Description, Rating (stars), Comment and the **thumbnail**.

Select several files (Ctrl or Shift + click) to edit them together. A field the files disagree on is empty with a
grey **Mixed** hint: leave it alone and each file keeps its own value; type in it and they all get what you typed.

For the picture, **From file…** picks a JPG or PNG, **From video frame** uses the frame under the **Source** monitor's
playhead (open the video there and park on the frame you like), and **Remove** takes it out.

**Save** writes everything into the files. Nothing is re-encoded: the sound and picture are copied as they are and
only the tags change. The new file is written next to the old one first; the old one is kept as `name.bak` until the
new one checks out, then removed. If a file is open in another program, you are told and it stays as it was.

![The Tags section in the Inspector](/img/tutorial/music-tags.png)

What each file type keeps: MP3, M4A / MP4 and FLAC keep every field and the cover. MP4 has no rating tag, FLAC folds
Description into Comment, WAV keeps the basic fields only, and Ogg / Opus / WAV files cannot hold a cover.

## Playlists

The **Playlists** panel is a tab beside the **Media Browser** on the **Media** page (on any other page, add it with a
tab bar's **+**).

- **New**, **Rename** and **Delete** at the top. Deleting a playlist never removes the songs from the Library.
- **Add songs** by dragging them from the Library onto the list, or select them in the Library and press **Add N
  selected**. Videos work too (music videos).
- **Reorder** with the grip on the left of a row (drag), or select a row and press **Alt+Up / Alt+Down**. The ✕ at the
  right removes a song.
- The footer shows the number of songs and the **total length**.
- **Gap** adds silence between songs; **Crossfade** makes them overlap and fade into each other instead.

Playlists are saved in the project.

- **▶ Play** (or the ▶ on a row, or a double-click) plays the playlist back to back in the **Source** monitor.
- **To timeline** puts the songs on the timeline at the playhead, back to back with the gap, or overlapping with
  fades when there is a crossfade: a mix.
- **Export .m3u8** saves the playlist for any music player (lengths and titles included; paths relative to the
  playlist file where they can be).

## Make a visualizer video

Pick a **Visualizer** style in the Playlists footer, tick **Cover art** and **Title card**, and press **Make
visualizer video**. Every song is put on the timeline with its own [audio visualizer](./audio-visualizers.mdx) layer
(linked to the song, so moving or trimming one moves the other), its cover art in the middle and its title and artist
underneath. Select songs in the Library and run **Make Visualizer Video** from the command palette (Ctrl+P) to do the
same without a playlist. Everything is one undo step.

## Burn to Disc

Press **Burn…** in the Playlists panel, or run **Burn to Disc…** from the command palette.

![The Burn to Disc window](/img/tutorial/music-burn.png)

- **Audio CD**: plays in any CD player. Each song is converted to CD audio (44.1 kHz, 16-bit stereo), with the gap you
  choose between tracks. The **Album** and **Artist** fields and each song's title go on as CD-Text where the burner
  supports it. The bar shows how much of the disc the music fills; an 80-minute CD holds 80:00, a 74-minute one 74:00,
  and a track is at least 4 seconds long.
- **Data disc**: files for a computer or a car stereo that plays MP3s. The playlist's songs go in a folder of their
  own with an `.m3u8` playlist, plus any files and folders you add. The disc works on Windows, macOS and Linux
  (ISO 9660 + Joliet + UDF).
- **Video DVD** is not offered: a DVD player needs a specially authored disc that Simple Editor cannot make. Put the
  video files on a data disc instead.

**Burn to** picks the drive, or **Disc image file** when you have no burner (or want to burn later): a data disc
becomes an `.iso` and an audio CD a `.bin` with its `.cue`, which any burning program (or Windows' own "Burn disc
image") can write.

With a drive picked, **In the drive** says what disc is in it (type, blank or not, free space). **Erase** quick-erases
a rewritable disc, **Eject** opens the tray, **Speed** picks the write speed, **Verify after burning** reads a data
disc back, and **Test only** does every check a burn does (drive, disc, space, the files) and writes nothing.

The burn runs in the background: it shows in the window and in the **Jobs** panel, where **Cancel** stops it. A burn
to a drive always leaves at least 2 seconds between audio tracks (that is how track-by-track burning works). Clear
messages tell you when there is no drive, no disc, the disc is not blank, or the music does not fit.
