![Project settings in the Inspector](/img/tutorial/project-settings.png)

Click an empty part of the timeline so nothing is selected, and the Inspector shows the project: **Name**,
**Format**, **Resolution**, **Frame rate**, **Color**, **Max length** and **Background**. The same rows are at
the top of the [Export](export.mdx) panel. An open nested sequence has its own.

## Frame rate

A project's frame rate is always a clean number. Phones and screen recorders often report rates like
26.0221…; Simple Editor snaps a measured rate to the nearest standard one within 3 % (23.976, 24, 25, 29.97,
30, 48, 50, 59.94, 60, 90, 120, 144 or 240) or rounds it to three decimals. A custom rate you type is rounded
the same way, and older projects are fixed when they open.

## Match project to this video?

![The Match project card](/img/tutorial/match-project.png)

When you import a video whose size, frame rate or HDR differs from the project (or the first video into an
empty project), a small card asks **Match project to this video?**:

- **Match** sets the project's size, frame rate and, for an HDR video, its colour to the video's, as one undo
  step.
- **Keep** leaves the project as it is.
- **Don't ask again** stops the card; **Settings ▸ General** turns it back on.

Importing many files at once asks only once, about all of them: with several formats it suggests one for the
mix (see [Mixing cameras and frame rates](mixing-cameras.mdx)).

## Color: SDR or HDR

| Color | What you get |
|---|---|
| **SDR Rec.709** | Standard video: looks the same on every screen and site. The default. |
| **HDR10 (Rec.2100 PQ)** | 10-bit HEVC (or AV1 for WebM) with PQ and HDR10 metadata, for HDR TVs, phones and YouTube HDR |
| **HDR HLG** | 10-bit HEVC (or AV1) with HLG: broadcast HDR that still plays as SDR on SDR screens |

In an HDR project, graphics, text and SDR footage sit at 203 nits, the usual "paper white". The Preview
shows HDR tone-mapped. The Export panel shows the colour space and tells you when a format can't carry it
(GIF, AVI or an 8-bit encoder).

HDR footage in any project is tone-mapped to SDR when it is decoded for the Preview, proxies, thumbnails and SDR
exports, so it no longer looks washed out.

## Max length

![Max length on the timeline](/img/tutorial/max-length.png)

**Max length** is for videos with a time limit (Shorts, Reels, TikTok). Tick it and type a length, or pick
one: 0:15, 0:30, 1:00, 1:30, 3:00, 10:00, 1 h, 2 h, 12 h. Longer lengths can be typed in any of these ways:
`9999h`, `1h 30m`, `2d 1:00:00`.

The timeline then draws a red line at the limit and shades everything after it, clips included, with
**over by 0:04** in its toolbar. Zoom to Fit and the scrollbar include the part past the line.

Nothing is cut. Exports stop at the limit, and the Export panel says so in one line with a button to include
the rest.
