---
title: Screen recording
description: Record a screen, a window, a region or one game or app, with its sound on separate audio tracks.
---

:::caution Experimental
Still being worked on; things may change or break.
:::

![The Screen Recording window](/img/tutorial/screen-recording.png)

**File ▸ Record ▸ Screen Recording…** (or **Window ▸ Screen Recording**) opens the Screen Recording panel
in its own window; drag its tab into any page to dock it like any other panel. At the top is a live preview of
exactly what will be recorded, cursor included, at 30 frames a second. Right-click it for **Pause preview** /
**Show preview**; it also pauses by itself whenever you can't see it. Under the preview are the level meters and
the big **Record** button, which becomes **Stop** with the time recorded.

The settings sit in four sections that remember whether they are open:

- **Sources**: **Screen** (one screen or all of them), **Window**, **Region**, or **Application / Game**. Application
  capture records one program's own picture (Roblox, Roblox Studio, games and other GPU apps), even when other
  windows cover it, and can record just that program's sound. **Audio** picks what **All audio** mixes: **System
  audio only** (what your computer plays, no microphone), **Microphone only**, **Both** or **None** (no "Stereo Mix"
  needed). **Audio tracks** lists the separate sound streams the file gets (by default All audio, Microphone and App
  audio), each with a name, an on/off switch and a meter; on the timeline each stream is its own audio track.
- **Quality**: frame rate, bitrate, scale and the encoder (CPU or graphics card).
- **Misc**: show the cursor; **Hide on Record**, which hides Simple Editor while you record and puts a small Stop pill
  at the top of the screen (it never shows in the recording); the **Stop hotkey** (Ctrl+Shift+F9 by default), which
  works in any app; and starting a recording when the editor loses focus.
- **Output**: the folder (**Open** shows it in Explorer), the **Library bin** the recording goes into (Recordings by
  default, or a new one), the file name (`{time}`, `{source}`) and whether it is added to the library at all.

When you stop, the recording is finished, checked, and then added to the Library. Recordings are written so they
can't be lost: if Simple Editor or the PC stops mid-recording, the pieces stay in the folder and are finished into
the mp4 the next time Simple Editor starts.

Recordings land in the Library as soon as they are finished, each sound stream on its own audio track when you
put the recording on the timeline. The Assistant can record too, with the `capture.screen` tool (open, start,
stop, status).
